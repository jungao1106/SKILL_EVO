\begin{abstract}
Software-engineering agents generate rich traces---issue interpretations, file
searches, edits, test runs, failures, and recoveries---that are discarded after
each benchmark trial. The obvious move is to recycle these traces into reusable
text skills, but the obvious move is also where most systems quietly cheat: raw
trace retrieval smuggles task-specific solutions into future prompts, and
one-shot ``summarize the trajectory'' pipelines emit over-specific or actively
harmful instructions that no one audits. Either way, apparent gains come from
memorization or noise rather than transfer. We present \method{}, a
verifier-grounded framework that evolves external skills for \emph{frozen}
coding agents while making leakage structurally hard. \method{} refuses to call
every trace artifact a skill: it separates single-task evidence from reusable
skills, promotes evidence through a hierarchy of repo-level and cross-repo
failure-mode skills, and trains separate Skill Writer and Skill Evaluator
policies behind a fixed harness policy. The design is built around one hard
information boundary. During SWEGym training the official verifier supplies
outcome labels, evaluator calibration, and validation gates; at downstream
evaluation the verifier never touches the update path---no hidden test, gold
patch, or future outcome may alter a skill. We instantiate the framework in a
runnable system (Pi harness, OpenAI-compatible models, Harbor/E2B execution,
versioned skill archives with promotion logs and validation gates) and specify
an evaluation protocol over a $2{\times}2$ executor matrix---GLM-5.2 and GPT-5.5
$\times$ Pi and Claude-style harnesses---that isolates model transfer, harness
transfer, and joint transfer, with SWE-bench Verified and DeepSWE as in-domain
targets and Terminal-Bench 2.0/2.1 as an out-of-domain extrapolation test, all
under a shared sandbox and acceptance rule.
\end{abstract}
